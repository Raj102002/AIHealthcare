## Two-Tiered Blood Testing

CDC recommends a standard two-step (two-tiered) blood testing process to help diagnose Lyme disease: a first-tier enzyme-linked immunoassay (ELISA) test, followed by a second, more specific Western blot test only if the first result is positive or equivocal. If the first test is negative, no further testing is recommended.

## Limitations of Testing

These tests detect antibodies to the Lyme disease bacteria, so results can be falsely negative in the first few weeks after infection, before the body has produced enough antibodies.

## Antibody Response Timing

Standard two-tier Lyme disease testing detects the body's antibody response to the infection, not the bacterium itself. The IgM antibody response typically peaks 3 to 6 weeks after infection and may not be detectable in the first two weeks. As a result, the sensitivity of testing at first presentation (early illness) is estimated at roughly 22-36%, meaning a real, active infection can still produce a negative test result if the test is drawn too early in the course of illness.

## Retesting Guidance

CDC recommends repeat testing 2 to 4 weeks later when the first test was taken before the body has had time to develop an adequate antibody response, so an early negative result is not the end of the process for someone with a compatible exposure and symptom history.
