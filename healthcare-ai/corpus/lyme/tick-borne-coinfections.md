## Why Co-Infection Matters

The same blacklegged tick that spreads Lyme disease can also carry other pathogens, including Babesia (which causes babesiosis) and Anaplasma (which causes anaplasmosis), as well as Borrelia miyamotoi. A person can be infected with more than one of these at the same time from a single tick bite. Untreated co-infection is a documented reason some patients do not fully improve after standard Lyme disease treatment, since Lyme antibiotics do not necessarily treat these other infections.

## Babesiosis Signals

Babesiosis symptoms can overlap with Lyme disease but also include some distinct signals: drenching night sweats (soaking through clothing or bedding) and air hunger (a sensation of not being able to get a full breath, distinct from ordinary shortness of breath). These are not symptoms typically emphasized in Lyme disease alone, and their presence is a reasonable question to raise with a clinician about testing for babesiosis.

## Anaplasmosis Signals

Anaplasmosis often presents with a high fever together with low blood cell counts (cytopenias, such as low white blood cells or platelets) found on routine bloodwork. A high fever combined with abnormal blood counts, especially following a known or possible tick exposure, is a reasonable question to raise with a clinician about testing for anaplasmosis.
